News & Catalyst Briefing

Moody's says 'unprecedented' AI spending threatens credit quality of Amazon, Meta, Alphabet and others
Moody's has warned that AI infrastructure spending is pushing even the most cash-rich technology companies to take on significant debt, issue stock, and employ off-balance-sheet financing arrangements. The scale of capital deployment is described as unprecedented, raising concerns about the long-term credit profiles of firms including Amazon, Meta, and Alphabet. The warning signals that rating agencies are beginning to scrutinize how major tech companies fund their AI ambitions alongside existing financial obligations.
Magnificent 7 stocks shed hundreds of billions amid AI spending fears
The Magnificent Seven group of large-cap technology stocks lost a combined $787 billion in market value in a single trading session following earnings reports from Alphabet and Tesla. Both companies disclosed massive capital expenditure plans tied to AI infrastructure, which unsettled investors concerned about near-term profitability and return on investment. The selloff represents one of the largest single-day valuation declines for the group since the AI investment cycle began accelerating.
The Fed's New Chairman Faces His Biggest Test Yet
Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin M. Warsh faces a critical policy decision this week as the Fed's rate-setting committee convenes for its latest meeting. Warsh has previously stated the Fed has no tolerance for elevated inflation, setting up a potential pivot toward tighter monetary policy. A decision to raise interest rates would mark a significant shift in the Fed's recent stance and would be the first major policy test of Warsh's chairmanship.
4 Big Tech earnings reports, a Fed meeting, and $100 oil: It's the busiest week of the quarter - Yahoo Finance
This week brings a convergence of major market-moving events, including earnings reports from four large technology companies, a Federal Reserve policy meeting, and oil prices hovering near the $100-per-barrel level. The combination of corporate earnings, central bank guidance, and commodity price pressure makes this one of the most consequential weeks of the third quarter. The outcomes across these events are expected to have broad implications for equity, bond, and energy markets.
Chile Taps International Bond Markets After Debt Limit Increase
Chile has returned to international debt markets for the second time in 2026, issuing sovereign bonds following a recent congressional vote to raise the country's limit on foreign bond sales. The move comes just weeks after lawmakers approved the higher debt ceiling, allowing the government to accelerate its borrowing program. The issuance reflects Chile's continued reliance on international capital markets to fund government expenditures amid evolving fiscal conditions.
