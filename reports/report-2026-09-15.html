News & Catalyst Briefing

Stock market today: Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq fall after 10-year Treasury yield rises
The Dow Jones Industrial Average, S&P 500, and Nasdaq all declined Tuesday as the 10-year Treasury yield moved higher. Rising yields increase borrowing costs and reduce the relative appeal of equities, particularly growth-oriented stocks. The broad-based selloff reflected investor sensitivity to interest rate movements across major indices.
Warsh's credibility is on the line this week as Trump policies put pressure on Fed to hike
Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh faces a critical test this week as inflation remains above the Fed's target and uncertainty persists around oil prices and tariff policy. Trump administration trade measures have complicated the inflation outlook, putting pressure on the Fed to consider rate hikes. Warsh's response to these conditions is being closely watched as a measure of his independence and policy judgment.
Carney Expands Tax Break to Broad Range of Investments
Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced an expansion of a major investment tax write-off to include oil and gas pipelines, mining property, and additional asset classes eligible for accelerated deductions. The move broadens a policy tool designed to stimulate capital investment across key sectors of the Canadian economy. The expansion signals the Carney government's intent to use the tax code to encourage domestic resource and infrastructure development.
Waystar Surges As Medical Software Maker Reportedly Mulls A Sale
Shares of Waystar surged Tuesday after reports emerged that the medical software company is exploring strategic options, including a potential sale. Waystar operates in the healthcare revenue cycle management software space, a sector that has attracted significant acquisition interest in recent years. The company has not confirmed the reports, and no deal terms or potential buyers have been publicly identified.
Sam Altman says OpenAI won't go public in 2026 amid AI safety concerns
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman stated that the company will not pursue an initial public offering in 2026, citing concerns about AI safety and alignment as reasons for the delay. OpenAI has been one of the most closely watched potential IPO candidates given its rapid growth and dominant position in the generative AI market. Altman indicated that resolving foundational safety questions takes priority over accessing public capital markets at this time.
